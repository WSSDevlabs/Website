The International Greentech and Eco Products Exhibition and Conference Malaysia (IGEM) ran its 17th edition at the Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre from **7 to 9 October 2026**. The Ministry of Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability (NRES) led it, with the Malaysian Green Technology and Climate Change Corporation (MGTC) as co-organiser, and it ran alongside the **Kuala Lumpur Sustainability Summit (KLSS) 2026**.

## The numbers going in

Before the show, organisers set a target of **RM22.5 billion in business potential**, against RM5.5 billion targeted the year before. They expected more than 60,000 visitors from over 50 countries and about 450 exhibitors.

Final results had not been published when we wrote this. Treat those figures as targets, and watch MGTC and NRES for the post-show report.


## Three signals for businesses that did not exhibit

**1. Green requirements are moving down the supply chain.** Large buyers and listed companies now report on emissions, and they pass questions to their suppliers. A factory or plantation that can show measured energy, fuel and waste data will find those conversations easier.

**2. Money follows measurement.** Budget 2027 extends investment tax allowances of up to 100% for green technology to 2030 and adds a RM1 billion KWAP Climate Fund. Green financing and incentives both need evidence: a baseline, a project and a measured result. We covered the details in [Budget 2027 and green energy](/insights/budget-2027-green-energy-incentives).

**3. The entry ticket is smaller than the show suggests.** Exhibition halls fill up with turbines, batteries and EV chargers. For most SMEs, the first step costs far less: meter energy by machine, check whether existing solar performs as it should, and log waste streams that could become feedstock.
