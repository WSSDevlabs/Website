The Malaysia Agriculture, Horticulture and Agrotourism show (MAHA) ran from **28 August to 6 September 2026** at the Malaysia Agro Exposition Park Serdang (MAEPS), which MARDI owns under the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security.

Organisers targeted four million visitors, 2,000 exhibitors and RM58 million in sales. The theme was **"Value Creation for Food Security"**, framed around a resilient, technology-driven agro-food sector. MAHA runs every two years, so the next edition falls around 2028.

## From theme to practice

"Technology-driven food security" sounds like drones and robots. On most Malaysian farms, the gains come from simpler things done consistently:

- **Know your weather.** A farm weather station costs less than one bad irrigation decision in a dry spell.
- **Measure the soil.** Moisture and nutrient probes tell you which blocks need water or fertiliser, so subsidised inputs go where they help.
- **Catch disease early.** A worker photographs a leaf, and an AI model flags the likely problem the same day.
- **Protect the boundary.** Cameras with animal detection warn your team before elephants, wild boar or monkeys reach the crop.
- **Keep records.** Block-by-block history of inputs, labour and harvest is what buyers, auditors and banks ask for.

## Why records matter more than gadgets

Budget 2027 gives the agriculture ministry [RM6.92 billion](/insights/budget-2027-agriculture-food-security-tech), most of it as subsidies and incentives. Farms that know their yields, costs and input use per block can show what that support achieves. Farms that don't can only estimate.

## A first project before next season

Choose the block or boundary that loses you the most money and instrument only that. Run it for one season. If the data changes what you do, extend it.
