"How much should I pay for a website?" is the question I get most often, and the honest answer, "it depends", isn't much help when you're the one signing the quote. So here are the real ranges Malaysian businesses are quoted in 2026, what actually moves the price, the costs that quotes often leave out, and, at the end, exactly what we charge.

A quick disclosure first: we're a web studio, so factor in our bias. Every range below comes from published Malaysian price guides, linked at the end, so you can check them yourself.

## The short answer

Most Malaysian SME websites cost between **RM500 and RM20,000**. Where you land depends mostly on how custom the work is and whether you sell online.

| Type of website | Typical 2026 range | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| Template or basic one-page site | RM500 to RM1,500 | New and very small businesses |
| Custom business website (5 to 10 pages) | RM3,000 to RM8,000 | Established SMEs that need enquiries |
| Corporate SME site from a Kuala Lumpur agency | RM8,000 to RM18,000 | Larger companies with more pages and approvals |
| Online store (e-commerce) | RM5,000 to RM20,000+ | Shops selling on their own site |

Large custom platforms, with logins, integrations and many user types, sit above these ranges and can pass RM50,000. Those are software projects rather than websites, and they should be scoped differently (more on that below).

These figures come from studios and agencies that publish their prices. They have an interest in their own numbers, so treat them as a guide, not official statistics.

## What actually moves the price

Page count is the thing everyone asks about, and it's the least important. These matter more:

- **Custom design or template.** A template-based site can ship in days. A custom design means a designer's time on concepts and revisions. Neither is wrong; just know which one you're buying.
- **Selling online.** Payments, stock, shipping rules and order emails roughly double the scope of a site.
- **Who writes the words.** Content is the most common cause of delays. If you can't write your own pages, budget for copywriting.
- **Revision rounds.** Two rounds on design and two on the build is common. "Unlimited revisions" sounds generous but usually means an unclear scope.
- **Integrations.** Booking systems, CRMs, accounting software and WhatsApp automation each add work.
- **Languages.** A Bahasa Malaysia and English site is close to building two sites' worth of content.
- **Who builds it.** Freelancers usually charge less, agencies more, because of their team and overheads. Small studios sit in between.

## The costs after launch (often missing from quotes)

A website keeps costing money after it goes live. Ask about these before you sign:

- **Domain:** roughly RM80 to RM200 a year for a .com or .my.
- **Hosting:** from around RM200 a year for a simple site, more for an online store, which needs more resources and backups.
- **Maintenance:** basic care plans for small business sites typically run RM150 to RM300 a month, covering updates, backups and small edits.
- **Payment gateway fees:** a percentage or fixed fee per transaction, charged by the gateway, if you sell online.
- **Your own time:** someone has to keep prices, photos and news up to date. A site with an easy editor (a CMS) makes this minutes instead of a developer's invoice.

A quote that only shows the first invoice isn't the full price. Ask what happens in month two and year two.

## Red flags in a website quote

- **Guaranteed Google rankings.** Nobody controls Google. A "guaranteed page 1" (or page 3) promise is a sign of a seller who either doesn't understand SEO or doesn't mind misleading you.
- **The domain in the developer's name.** Your domain should be registered in your name, from day one.
- **No renewal prices.** If the quote doesn't say what hosting and the domain cost in year two, assume the number will surprise you.
- **"Fully custom" at a template price.** Very low prices for a "custom, full-featured website" usually mean a template. That's fine, as long as it's what you want.
- **No exit.** If you can't get your files, logins and content out, you're renting, not owning.

## Seven questions to ask before you sign

1. Is the domain registered in my name?
2. What exactly is included, and what isn't?
3. How many revision rounds do I get?
4. What will I pay from year two?
5. Can I edit the text and photos myself?
6. Once I've paid, do I own the design, code and content?
7. If you're not around next year, what do I receive so someone else can take over?

A good developer answers all seven in writing, without hesitation.

## When a "website" is really a software project

If what you need has logins, approvals, staff accounts or links to your accounting system, you're no longer buying a website. You're commissioning software. The biggest risk there is an unclear scope: you imagine one thing, the developer builds another. The fix is a short, paid discovery phase that ends in a written requirements document and a fixed price, before any building starts.
