The monsoon season starts in a few weeks, and Budget 2027 puts real money into drainage, water and public space. Here is what was announced, and where monitoring technology helps the money go further.

## Flood mitigation

- **RM100 million** for retention ponds and river maintenance in the Klang Valley.
- The capacity of the **Batu Dam and Klang Gate Dam** will be assessed for the next 30 years.
- Planning begins on the **Lebir Dam** in Kelantan as a long-term solution.
- New flood projects for 2027 in **Kuching City, Sungai Golok and Sungai Kemaman**.
- Close to **RM320 million** for slopes, drains and ditches maintained by local authorities.

## Water and public space

The Non-Revenue Water programme gets **RM2.5 billion**, focused on replacing 1,900km of old pipes. In Kuala Lumpur, DBKL plans **22km of covered walkways** linking landmarks, housing, shops and public transport, and **RM400 million** to create 120 acres of new green space and repair parks. A 100-acre site in Bukit Kiara is to be gazetted as a federal park.

## Where sensors and data help

Infrastructure money buys concrete. Monitoring tells you whether the concrete works and where to spend next.

- **Water-level sensors** in drains, retention ponds and rivers, with alerts to operators and residents when levels pass a set mark.
- **CCTV on known hotspots** to spot blocked drains and rising water, using cameras councils already own.
- **Pressure and flow sensors** on water networks to find leaks faster, which is the whole point of a non-revenue water programme.
- **Smart lighting** on new walkways and parks that reports its own faults and dims when paths are empty.
- **A digital twin** of a township or district that brings drainage, lighting, complaints and maintenance onto one map.

None of this replaces the civil works. It helps councils, township managers and developers see problems before residents post them online, and plan the next round of spending on evidence.
