KL20, Malaysia's flagship startup summit, runs as a three-city series in 2026. It started in **Penang on 24 September** with a focus on semiconductors and electrical and electronics, moved to **Johor in late September** for the creative economy and digital gaming, and finishes in **Kuala Lumpur on 5 November**.

## Eight sectors instead of four

The 2024 summit focused on four sectors. The 2026 programme keeps them and adds four more:

| Original four | Added in 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Green technology | Creative economy |
| Agritech | Digital economy |
| Manufacturing and automation | Digital gaming |
| Islamic finance | Third sector |

The Kuala Lumpur finale covers technology talent, capital, market access and startup infrastructure. Attendance is by invitation, unlike 2024. The wider goal, [reported by The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/08/07/malaysia-aims-for-top-20-global-startup-ranking-by-2030), is a place in the top 20 global startup ecosystems by 2030.

## What the list tells you

Three of the eight sectors, **green technology, agritech and manufacturing and automation**, need software and hardware working together: sensors in the field, models that read the data and dashboards people trust. That combination is harder to build than an app, and Malaysia needs more teams who can deliver it end to end.

For established SMEs, the message is about customers as much as startups. Government attention and investor money in these sectors usually turn into procurement, pilots and supplier programmes over the next two years. Businesses with clean data and a working digital setup will be easier to partner with.

## If you are building in these sectors

- **Start with a measurable problem**, not a technology. "We lose X tonnes to disease" beats "we want AI".
- **Pilot on one site** and collect your own evidence before you pitch or scale.
- **Plan for the field.** Power, connectivity and dust decide whether a system survives outside the demo room.
