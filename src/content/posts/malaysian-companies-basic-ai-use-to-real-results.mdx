An opinion piece in The Edge Malaysia, citing AWS-commissioned research, reports that roughly **27% of Malaysian companies had adopted AI by 2024**, up from 20% a year earlier. The same piece notes that around **73% of businesses remain at a basic level** of AI use, with only about 10% applying it in more advanced, transformative ways.

In other words, many of us have tried AI. Far fewer have made it change how the business runs.

## What "basic" usually looks like

Typing questions into a chat tool, drafting a message or summarising a document. Useful, but it lives on one person's screen and disappears when they close the tab.

## What moving past it looks like

- **It is connected to your data.** The tool works from your own orders, records or documents, not from a generic guess.
- **It is part of a routine.** It runs every day without anyone having to remember to use it.
- **It has an owner and a number.** Someone watches one measure, such as time saved or mistakes caught, so you know it is helping.

A minister recently told SMEs, as [reported by The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/10/09/smes-must-embrace-ai-to-stay-competitive-says-sim), that being an AI company does not mean just buying an AI system. Firms also need data systems, talent and safeguards.

## A simple way to start

Choose one repetitive task. Write down how long it takes each week. Try a small automation on that task alone for a few weeks, and compare. If it earns its keep, grow it. If not, you learned cheaply.
