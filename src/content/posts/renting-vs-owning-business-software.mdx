Most small businesses in Malaysia now pay monthly for their software: the POS, the attendance app, the booking tool, the website builder. Each bill looks small. Together they never stop. So is it cheaper to buy software once instead?

Sometimes. Not always. This guide works through a real example, including the cases where renting is the better deal, so you can decide with your own numbers.

A disclosure first: we sell software you buy once, so factor in our bias. That's exactly why the comparison below includes the costs of owning that sellers like us tend to leave out.

## What a subscription really includes

To be fair to subscriptions, a monthly fee usually covers more than the software:

- **Hosting:** the servers the software runs on.
- **Updates:** security patches and new features, installed for you.
- **Support:** someone to call when something goes wrong.
- **Backups:** usually automatic.

When you own software, you take on some of these yourself, or you pay someone to handle them. Any honest comparison has to count them.

The hosting figure is an estimate; your host may charge more or less. Three things stand out.

**1. Owning, without a support plan, costs far less.** If you or someone on your team can handle hosting and install updates using a guide, owning costs about 40% less than renting over three years, and the system keeps running in year four, five and beyond with only hosting to pay.

**2. Owning with full support can cost more for a single outlet.** If you want someone else to look after everything (backups, updates, support), a monthly care plan on top of hosting can add up to more than one subscription. That's not a trick of the numbers. Support is real work, and it costs money whoever provides it.

**3. The picture changes fast with more outlets.** Subscriptions are usually charged per outlet, per till or per user. A system you own on one installation can serve several outlets, while one care plan covers that one installation. At three outlets, the same subscription costs three times as much, while the cost of owning one installation stays much the same.

## When renting is the better choice

Renting makes sense when:

- you need the software for a short time, or you're still testing whether a new business will work;
- you have one small location, no technical help at all, and you want everything handled for you;
- the provider's features (for example, a large app marketplace or deep integrations) are worth the monthly fee to you; or
- cash flow matters more than total cost: a monthly fee is easier to start than a one-time payment.

## When owning is the better choice

Owning makes sense when:

- your process is stable and you'll use the system for years;
- you have several outlets, tills or users and are paying per seat;
- you want your data on your own server, under your control, for example because of the personal data you hold under the PDPA;
- you want to change the system to fit how you work, which needs the source code; or
- you don't want to lose access if you stop paying, or if the provider raises prices or shuts down.

## The hidden costs on both sides

**Hidden costs of renting:**

- price rises you can't negotiate;
- per-user or per-outlet fees that grow with your business;
- add-ons for features you assumed were included;
- lock-in: exporting your data can be hard, and you lose access when you stop paying; and
- the risk of the provider changing direction, being acquired or closing.

**Hidden costs of owning:**

- hosting, which you pay to your host directly;
- installation, if you can't do it yourself;
- someone to handle updates, backups and problems, whether that's you, your IT person or a care plan; and
- the upfront payment, which hits cash flow harder than a monthly fee.

## Questions to ask before you decide

1. How long will I realistically use this system?
2. How many outlets, tills or users will I have in two years?
3. Who will handle updates and backups if I own it?
4. What happens to my data if I stop paying, or the provider closes?
5. Can I get my data out in a format another system can read?
6. Do I need to change the software to fit how we work?

If your answers point to "years", "growing", "my own data" and "we'd like to customise it", owning is likely the better deal. If they point to "short term", "one location" and "I want everything handled", renting may well be.
